# Short notices and disclaimers

Each pattern: where it goes, the wording, what backs it, and what must be
true first. Adapt the words to the product's voice; keep the substance.

## Tone

A notice is read by someone in the middle of doing something. Write it for
that person, not for a court.

| Harsh (avoid)                                       | Plain (use)                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Uploading copyrighted material is strictly prohibited. | Only upload pictures and videos you own or have permission to show.     |
| Will a static menu damage my TV?                    | Can my TV show the same picture all day?                                  |
| WARNING: We are not liable for ...                  | (Leave liability to the terms; the notice links them.)                    |
| You must not ...                                    | Please ... / Only ...                                                      |

- Lead with what to do. Put the reassurance first when the true answer is
  reassuring, then the caveat for the few it applies to.
- Do not raise a risk the reader had no reason to think about in the first
  thing they read (a first FAQ entry, a first-run screen). Answer it where
  someone who has the question will look.
- One link to the clause, labelled with the clause ("Terms §6").

## Upload rights

- **Where:** beside every control that picks a file to upload.
- **Text:** "Only upload pictures and videos you own or have permission to
  show. Terms §6" (link to the content clause, new tab).
- **Backed by:** a terms clause where the customer confirms they hold the
  rights to what they upload, and an acceptable-use line against content
  they have no rights to.
- **Not:** a checkbox per upload.

## Who is responsible for what is shown or published

- **Where:** in the terms ("Your content" or similar), not in the UI.
- **Clause:** the customer owns their content and is responsible for it,
  including accuracy and any notices their jurisdiction requires; we store,
  process and deliver it only to run the service; we do not review it; the
  customer indemnifies us for claims arising from it.
- **Note:** this places responsibility on the customer between the two of
  you. It does not stop a rights holder from writing to the service that
  stores the copies; that is what the copyright-complaint process is for.

## Copyright complaints (DMCA notice-and-takedown)

- **Where:** a terms clause (appended), and a contact address for the
  designated agent.
- **Precondition:** the owner registers a designated agent with the US
  Copyright Office and keeps it current (renewal every three years). Without
  it, do not publish the clause as if the safe harbour applies.
- **The notice must contain** (17 U.S.C. §512(c)(3)): the complainant's
  physical or electronic signature; the work claimed to be infringed; the
  material claimed to infringe and enough to locate it; the complainant's
  contact details; a statement of good-faith belief that the use is not
  authorised; and a statement that the notice is accurate and, under
  penalty of perjury, that the complainant is authorised to act.
- **Also state:** the counter-notice route for the customer, and that
  repeat infringers' accounts are ended in appropriate circumstances.
- **Lawyer review:** yes, once.

## Trademarks and "not affiliated"

- **Where:** near any mention of another company's product (a device you
  recommend, a store you link to), and as a clause in the terms.
- **Text:** "<Brand> is a trademark of <Owner>. <Product> is not
  affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by <Owner>." Link the
  trademark clause.

## Affiliate or referral links

- **Where:** next to the link, before the reader clicks.
- **Text:** "We may earn a commission if you buy through this link." Add
  "It does not change the price." only when that is true. Required (US
  FTC) once a link pays you; while links are unpaid, say nothing or say
  they are unaffiliated.

## Illustrative examples

- **Where:** under generated or staged pictures that a reader could take
  for a real customer or a real product screen.
- **Text:** "Illustrative settings and sample screen content." or
  "Illustrative example."

## Agreement at sign-up (clickwrap)

- **Where:** directly beside or above the sign-up button, before it.
- **Text:** "By creating an account, you agree to the Terms and acknowledge
  the Privacy Policy." Both linked, visible without scrolling, and in a
  readable size. A button that names the act ("Agree and create account")
  or an unticked checkbox makes the act clearer still.
- **Precondition:** the terms and privacy pages exist and are current.
  Record which version the account agreed to if the product can.

## Marketing consent

- **Where:** a separate, unticked checkbox, never bundled with the terms.
- **Text:** "Send me product news and tips. You can unsubscribe at any
  time."
- **Note:** opt-in is required for EU/UK recipients; US law (CAN-SPAM)
  allows opt-out, but opt-in is the simpler single rule.

## Email footer

- **Commercial mail** (US CAN-SPAM): the sender's identity, a valid postal
  address, and a working unsubscribe honoured promptly. Gmail and Yahoo
  also require one-click unsubscribe headers from bulk senders.
- **Transactional mail** (receipts, password resets, security): no
  unsubscribe from what the account needs; naming the legal entity in the
  footer is still good practice.

## Not professional advice

- **Where:** on content that looks like advice (calculators, guides, FAQ
  answers about law, tax, health or money).
- **Text:** "General information, not legal advice." (or tax, medical,
  financial). Keep it to the content it covers.

## AI-generated content

- **Where:** beside text, images or answers the product generates.
- **Text:** "Made with AI. Check it before you rely on it." For generated
  pictures of people or places, label them as illustrations.
